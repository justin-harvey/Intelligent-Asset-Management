Product Direction

What we're building

One product underneath two wedges: a cross-vendor, cross-asset coordinator of the maintenance an operator still owns.

The product in one sentence

An AI-native orchestration layer that detects a fault, triages it, dispatches a qualified actor, verifies the fix, and updates the asset record, across every vendor and asset class the operator runs, not just one manufacturer's equipment.

It is not a CMMS/EAM system of record, and it is not maintenance labor. It sits above the systems of record and across the single-vendor monitoring tools, closing the loop that none of them close: turning a condition signal into a verified restoration against an SLA.

The core loop

1 · Detect: ingest fault / condition signals from mixed hardware and telemetry (the "live pulse").
2 · Triage: classify, prioritize against the SLA, and deduplicate noise.
3 · Dispatch: route the right qualified actor (field tech, vendor, internal crew) with the context to fix it first-time.
4 · Verify: confirm the asset is actually restored to good condition, not just "ticket closed."
5 · Record: write verified evidence back to the asset record, the proof that compounds into an operator-controlled condition history.
The one seam incumbents don't hold

RaaS vendors and OEMs own maintenance inside their own fleet. Systems of record (Maximo, SAP) store the data but don't coordinate the fix. Single-OEM monitoring (e.g. Konecranes TRUCONNECT) covers one manufacturer. The defensible product is cross-vendor, cross-asset-class coordination of maintenance the operator still owns; everything single-vendor or RaaS-captured is a trap.

Positioning: vertical SaaS, not a horizontal platform

Same engine underneath, sold as a vertical product. Two reasons:

  • Multiple premium. Vertical B2B SaaS trades at ~8.1× revenue vs ~5.2× horizontal, a 1.5–2× premium for entering vertical.
  • It dodges the "trenchcoat" trap. A horizontal platform that is really N bespoke integrations wearing a trenchcoat dies on integration cost. We earn the right to generalize only after the engine is proven in one vertical.

Guardrails

Carry these forward; they are easy to violate
  • Sell the orchestration layer, not the wrenching. Addressable market is the software slice, not MRO spend or the $49B digital-twin TAM.
  • Flip the data play. Operators prove good condition to lower their own cost of capital; do not assume resale of adverse condition data.
  • Residential stays a late-horizon / narrative device, not a near-term paid profile.
  • Beware the horizontal trenchcoat. A reusable layer must transfer across the vertical without becoming N bespoke integrations.
