Product Architecture

How it works

A thin, vendor-neutral layer over the systems that already exist: read mixed signals in, coordinate a verified repair, write evidence the operator owns.

Principles

  • Operator-owned data. The condition record belongs to the operator; we never resell it. This is the flipped data play, provable good condition to lower their own cost of capital.
  • Vendor-neutral by construction. A canonical asset/fault model with thin adapters, so adding a CPMS or OEM is configuration, not a new codebase. This is how we avoid the "trenchcoat" of N bespoke integrations.
  • Labor-agnostic. We coordinate the operator's own crews and any vendor; we don't own the wrench.
  • Verified restoration, not "ticket closed." The loop isn't done until the asset is confirmed back to good condition.
  • Integrate, don't replace. We sit above the system of record and across the network tools, not in place of them.

The layer, end to end

How we handle every brand without drowning in custom work

Plain English Every brand of equipment "speaks" differently. Instead of writing custom code for each one, we translate them all into a single shared language the moment their data comes in. After that, everything we build works for every brand automatically. Adding a new brand is like adding a dictionary entry, not rebuilding the software.

The thing that kills a "horizontal" layer is bespoke integration cost. We defuse it with a canonical model: every source is mapped to one internal representation of an asset, a fault, and a restoration event. Adapters are thin and mostly declarative, onboarding a new CPMS or OEM is a mapping config, not a new engine. The core logic (triage, dispatch, verify) is written once against the canonical model and reused across every vendor and asset class.

Where the AI actually sits

Specific jobs, not "AI magic":

  • Remote diagnosis before dispatch, the single biggest lever on repair time per industry data. Resolve or correctly classify the fault before a truck rolls.
  • Triage & prioritization against the SLA, deduplicating noisy or duplicate faults across sources.
  • Dispatch routing, matching the fault to the right qualified actor with the context to fix it first-time.
  • Verification & anomaly detection, confirming restoration and flagging "closed but not actually fixed."
  • Evidence summarization, turning the record into a compliance-ready uptime narrative.

Data & security posture

Plain English Each customer's data is walled off and stays theirs; we never sell it or mix it with anyone else's. We mostly just read equipment status and write back as little as possible. We don't collect or sell personal information, we make money from the coordination and the compliance records, not from data.

  • Tenant-isolated, operator-owned. Each operator's data is theirs; no cross-operator resale.
  • Read-mostly ingestion via OCPP and vendor APIs, minimal write-back to systems of record and FSM.
  • No PII resale. The business model is the coordination layer and the compliance evidence, not data brokerage.
  • Compliance-grade audit trail with export built for the NEVI reporting fields (see the NEVI mandate).

What we deliberately don't build

Not a CPMS. Not hardware. Not a labor marketplace. Not a new system of record. Each of those has entrenched players; our value is the coordination seam between them, which no one owns.
