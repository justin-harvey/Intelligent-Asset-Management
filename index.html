Intelligent Asset Lifecycle Orchestration

One piece of software that keeps mixed-brand equipment running: it spots the fault, sends the fix, and proves the repair held. We start in EV charging; ports are our proving ground.

In plain English

When equipment breaks, a public EV charger, or a crane at a shipping port, someone has to notice, send the right person, and confirm it's truly fixed. Today that is slow, manual, and split across different brands of gear that don't talk to each other. We're building one piece of software that watches all of it no matter who made it, dispatches the repair, and proves it held. We start with EV charging because a new federal rule (NEVI) now requires public chargers to work more than 97% of the time, so keeping them up is the law, not a nice-to-have.

Bottom line up front

One product, two possible ways in. Our lead bet is EV-charging networks: keeping chargers working is a top operator problem, the law now pushes it (NEVI's 97% rule), and no existing system does the job. We'd aim to prove the hard part, coordinating many brands and many kinds of equipment at once, at a port terminal. We don't have either foothold yet; both are bets we'd have to win, and EV in particular may be a long shot.

The payoff: get acquired. Near-term by an active EV-charging buyer (ABB, Nayax, ChargePoint); longer-term by one of the big equipment-software consolidators (IFS, Hexagon, IBM).

Where this stands: an early-stage idea with a working prototype (MVP). It is not funded and not yet running in a real port. The next step is market outreach to EV-charging and port operators to confirm the need and build interest. That interest is what funds the real build and the custom work each customer needs.
