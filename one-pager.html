Intelligent Asset Lifecycle Orchestration · Confidential

The coordination layer that keeps assets up

We turn "a charger is down" into "the asset is verified repaired," across every vendor, so operators hit the uptime they are now required by law to deliver.

Problem

EV-charging reliability is the industry's #1 problem, real first-time charging success is ~71%, yet NEVI now mandates >97% uptime per port. The CPMS tells you a charger is down; nothing coordinates the cross-vendor repair to verified restoration.

Solution

A vendor-neutral, operator-owned layer: detect → triage → dispatch → verify → record, across mixed hardware and, uniquely, across asset classes (a port estate, not just chargers). Vertical SaaS, integrates with the stack rather than replacing it.

Why now

NEVI's 97% mandate converts maintenance into a funded compliance obligation with a named owner, and no incumbent maintenance system exists for it. Demand is pushed by law.

Market

~260k US public ports today, ~500k by 2030. EV-charging software-slice SAM ~$60–120M, the wedge, not the destination. Cross-asset expansion (ports, grid-DER) scales it.

Wedge & model

Aim first at EV-charging reliability, and prove the hard engine at a port terminal if we can secure a design partner; neither foothold exists yet. Per-active-port annual subscription; land-and-expand by ports and asset classes.

Why we win

CPMS vendors optimize their own network; RaaS owns the labor. We are the one cross-vendor, operator-owned, cross-asset coordinator, the O&M seam the industry says the CPMS can't fill.

Exit

Near-term strategic floor among active EV-charging buyers (ABB, Nayax, ChargePoint; recent tuck-ins $26–38M). Scaled target among EAM consolidators rolling up "end-to-end asset lifecycle" (IFS/Copperleaf $742M, Hexagon, IBM). Vertical SaaS ~8× revenue.

Status

Early-stage idea with a working prototype (MVP). Not funded; not yet live in a real port. The research (wedge and exit) is mapped; product-market fit is not yet proven.

Next step

Market outreach to EV-charging and port operators to confirm the need and sign a first design partner. That interest is what funds the real build and per-customer development.
