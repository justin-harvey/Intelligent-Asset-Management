Market Thesis

Why this market, and why here

A top-down, demand-first scan of eleven industries, backcast from an acquisition — then a break-point test that re-sorted them.

Method — backcast from the exit

Justify demand macro-first; let justified demand name the entry point; validate on the ground; design the build to land in an acquirer's path by ~2031.

The causal engine, applied to every industry:

macro capital wave → forced automation → a dense installed base → that base needs automated maintenance → our orchestration layer.

The decisive filter is the break point — the link where an automation vendor already owns the maintenance loop (via Robotics-as-a-Service or OEM "cost-per-hour" contracts), leaving no seam for an independent orchestrator. A chain either survives its own break-point test or is dropped.

The break-point test — the cross-cutting kill risk

The "horizontal trenchcoat" risk shows up concretely as the automation vendor already owns the maintenance layer — via RaaS, OEM remote-monitoring, or outcome-based "cost-per-hour" contracts. Where that holds, there is no seam for an independent orchestrator.

What survives

The one seam the RaaS and OEM incumbents don't hold is cross-vendor, cross-asset-class coordination of maintenance the operator still owns. EV charging is that seam at the network level (mixed hardware, slow dispatch, no diagnostics on 83% of faults). Ports is that seam at the estate level (multi-OEM cranes + rail + pavement + utilities that no single OEM monitoring tool spans).

The software slice we actually sell (TAM anchors)

Discipline from the guardrails: the addressable market is the orchestration/software slice — not MRO spend or the $49B digital-twin TAM. Figures are third-party research-firm estimates, directional.

  • Predictive maintenance: ~$11–13B (2024) → $30–56B by 2030, CAGR ~27–28%.
  • Enterprise Asset Management (EAM): ~$6.6–9.4B (2026), CAGR ~11–14% — and fragmented: IFS 9.4%, IBM/Maximo ~9%, ABB 8.4%, SAP 8.3%. The top four hold only ~35%. No dominant defendant at the orchestration layer.
  • EV charging (primary wedge): NA ~$10B by 2030 (~21% CAGR); global $94–227B by 2030 (30–37% CAGR); NEVI goal 500k chargers by 2030. Smaller absolute base is the only knock — tested via the expansion path.
  • Ports / terminal automation (beachhead): ~$11B → $16–19B by 2030 (~6–7% CAGR).

What the live data changed

  • The reshoring/fab thesis is softer than it looked. Manufacturing construction spend peaked ~Sept 2024 and is down 22–58%. The causal chain still holds long-term; the timing is wrong for a 5-year-exit wedge.
  • EV charging emerged as the standout — NEVI's 97% uptime mandate converts "maintenance" into a funded compliance obligation in a market with no incumbent maintenance system.
  • "No dominant defendant" is empirically supported — the EAM leader holds <10% share; the orchestration layer specifically is unclaimed.
  • Data centers are the value trap — highest capex, but you'd be displacing a mature DCIM stack.
