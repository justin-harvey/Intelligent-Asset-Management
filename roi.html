2 · Repair time is the lever

Industry data: best-in-class operators repair in under 5 hours; typical DC fast-charger repairs can stretch to weeks when parts and diagnosis are uncoordinated. Remote diagnosis before dispatch is the single most impactful factor in reducing repair time, exactly the detect→triage step of our engine. Less time down is simultaneously more revenue and more uptime.

3 · Downtime → the NEVI number

Using the mandate's own formula (525,600 minutes/year, see NEVI). To stay above 97%, a port can be down at most ~3% of the year = ~10.9 days.

ScenarioDowntime/yrUptime μNEVI 97%?
4 incidents, ~10 days each (slow, uncoordinated)40 days89.0%FAIL
Threshold10.9 days97.0%line
Same 4 incidents, ~2 days each (coordinated + remote diagnosis)8 days98.5%PASS

Cutting mean-time-to-repair from ~10 days to ~2 days per incident takes this port from 89% (non-compliant) to 98.5% (compliant), the difference between clawback exposure and a clean report.

Illustrative hard-dollar value before compliance and cost-of-capital effects: ~$4,000–5,000 per port per year. For a network of even a few hundred ports, that is a seven-figure annual value pool, most of which is currently unaddressed.

5 · Pricing sanity check

Pricing is a separate decision (a known gap), but the model sets the ceiling: if a placeholder price of $200–600 per port per year captures a fraction of $4,000–5,000 of value, the value-to-price ratio is strongly positive and the budget line (NEVI compliance / O&M) already exists. That is a painkiller, not a vitamin.
