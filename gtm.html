The motion

1 · Validate Problem-first interviews (M6), no selling. Confirm reliability/dispatch is a top-3, funded, incumbent-can't-solve problem.
2 · Design partner Turn the best-fit operator into a design partner: free during build, their problems shape the product, they get it first.
3 · Paid pilot One narrow workflow (fault → verified restoration) on a slice of ports, priced on the Compliance tier, with a baseline captured up front.
4 · Prove Document the uptime lift and MTTR drop against that baseline. This is the reference that sells the next logo.
5 · Expand More ports, then more asset classes, then more sites, lifting net revenue retention without new logos.

Channels

  • Direct, targeted. NEVI awardee lists + LinkedIn (VP Ops / Head of Reliability) at operators outside the big three. See sourcing & scripts.
  • Industry rooms. SEPA, reliability working groups, and the public reliability discourse (Paren, EVChargerScout) where operators air the pain.
  • CPMS partnerships. Integrate with AMPECO / ChargeLab / Driivz as signal sources and co-sell the layer they don't provide, rather than compete inside one network.
  • Field-service partners. Use RaaS/FSM players (incl. ChargerHelp) as one fulfillment option under our coordination, a partner channel, not a rival.

The ports beachhead, in parallel

EV charging supplies the funded market; the port terminal supplies the hardest technical proof (cross-OEM, cross-asset) and a marquee reference. The ports motion runs alongside the EV-charging motion, not after it, because it de-risks the generalization thesis the exit depends on.

What we must prove, and when

Each GTM stage maps to a capability on the backcast: Y1 a paying CPO + signed ports partner; Y2 multiple CPOs + a documented uptime lift; Y3 segment leadership + a live ports reference; Y4 a second vertical. The metric that unlocks everything downstream is a credible before/after on the uptime number, which is why the baseline capture in M6 is non-negotiable.
