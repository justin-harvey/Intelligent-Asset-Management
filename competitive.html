Competitive Teardown

Who else is in the loop, and why we win

The EV-charging stack already has a management layer, a repair layer, and a system of record. None of them closes the loop between "charger down" and "charger verified repaired" across vendors. That seam is the product.

The seam, confirmed by the market

Industry analysts in 2026 are explicit: operators "need O&M tools, not just a CPMS, to hit 97%," and the smartest operators now "layer specialized field-service management beneath their CPMS to close the gap between 'charger offline' and 'charger repaired.'" We are that layer, made cross-vendor, operator-owned, and cross-asset, so it also spans a port estate, not just one charging network.

The two real threats, and why we still win

Threat 1: CPMS vendors move into automated ops

AMPECO's CoOperator and ChargeLab's Spark show the CPMS layer is adding AI operations. This is the most credible path to our seam.

Why we win: each optimizes its own network. Our wedge is the long-tail and mixed-hardware operator who runs chargers across several CPMS vendors, where no single platform spans them, and the coordination layer must be vendor-neutral and operator-owned. And we don't stop at chargers, the same engine coordinates a port estate (cranes, rail, utilities), which no charging CPMS will ever do. We integrate with CoOperator/Spark as sources, not compete inside one network.

Threat 2: ChargerHelp and the RaaS model

ChargerHelp is the closest thing to us: reliability-focused, platform + field techs. But it is Reliability-as-a-Service, it owns and sells the labor.

Why we win (and why it may be a partner/acquirer): our M2 analysis holds, bundling the labor captures one operator's loop and doesn't travel cross-estate. We are the coordinator, not the labor pool: we orchestrate the operator's own crews and any vendor, including ChargerHelp as one fulfillment option. That keeps us labor-light, cross-network, and cross-asset. A RaaS player wanting a vendor-neutral software layer is a natural partner, and a plausible acquirer.
