Where to enter — and who buys us

A top-down, 5-year market scan of eleven industries, backcast from an acquisition, for the Intelligent Asset Lifecycle Orchestration platform.

Bottom line up front

Enter through a dual wedge, one product. Lead with EV-charging networks — demand is funded by law (NEVI's 97% uptime mandate), maintenance is operator-owned and unserved, and there is no incumbent maintenance system to displace. Prove the hard engine — cross-OEM, cross-asset coordination — at a port terminal, where we have direct access and a reference logo is cheap.

Underneath both: the same product — a cross-vendor, cross-asset coordinator of operator-owned maintenance. Position it as vertical SaaS, not a horizontal platform, for a 1.5–2× exit-multiple premium.

Exit: a near-term strategic floor among active EV-charging buyers (ABB, Nayax, ChargePoint), and a scaled target among the EAM consolidators rolling up "end-to-end asset lifecycle" (IFS, Hexagon, IBM).

Method — backcast from the exit

Justify demand macro-first; let justified demand name the entry point; validate on the ground; design the build to land in an acquirer's path by ~2031.

The causal engine, applied to every industry: macro capital wave → forced automation → a dense installed base → that base needs automated maintenance → our orchestration layer. The decisive filter is the break point — the link where an automation vendor already owns the maintenance loop (via Robotics-as-a-Service or OEM "cost-per-hour" contracts), leaving no seam for an independent orchestrator.

The defensible product

What survives the break-point test

The one seam the RaaS and OEM incumbents don't hold is cross-vendor, cross-asset-class coordination of maintenance the operator still owns. EV charging is that seam at the network level (mixed hardware, slow dispatch, no diagnostics on 83% of faults). Ports is that seam at the estate level (multi-OEM cranes + rail + pavement + utilities that no single OEM monitoring tool spans). Everything single-vendor or RaaS-captured is a trap.
