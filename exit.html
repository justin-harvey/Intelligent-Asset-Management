Exit Thesis

Who buys us, why, and at what multiple

Two credible exit paths: a near-term strategic floor in EV charging, and a scaled target among the EAM consolidators building "end-to-end asset lifecycle."

What we'd sell for, and why

Plain English Buyers price software like us as a multiple of its yearly revenue, so $10M of revenue at "8 times" sells for about $80M. Software built for one specific industry sells for a higher multiple than generic software. "Comps" are recent real deals we point to as proof of the going rate.

The multipleVertical B2B SaaS ≈ 8.1× revenue vs horizontal ≈ 5.2×, a 1.5–2× premium for entering vertical. The direct argument for the wedge-first strategy.
ContextIndustrial software ≈ 10.8× revenue (Sept 2026); SaaS M&A median ≈ 3.8× revenue, median deal ≈ $80M; strong private SaaS 8–10× ARR.
Anchor compsIFS → Copperleaf $742M sets the "scaled asset-lifecycle platform" ceiling; Lynkwell $25.9M and Flipturn $38.4M set the "early strategic exit" floor.

The big exit: the category consolidators

The EAM field is actively rolling up into "end-to-end asset lifecycle", our exact category:

  • IFS, serial acquirer: Copperleaf ($742M, 2024) + TheLoops (2025, autonomous AI agents). The clearest "buy our way to end-to-end asset lifecycle" strategy.
  • Hexagon, spun Octave (2025), consolidating Asset Lifecycle Intelligence + adjacent software.
  • IBM/Maximo, SAP, Siemens, AspenTech/Emerson, all hold asset-management franchises to extend.

Read: these are the acquirers who'd pay the Copperleaf-style multiple for a proven cross-vendor orchestration layer. They are the 3–5 year target, after the vertical wedge is proven.

The exit thesis, in sequence

1 · Enter vertical EV charging, for the demand and the ~1.5–2× vertical-SaaS multiple premium.
2 · Prove the engine Ports (cross-OEM/cross-asset) via direct access → the hard technical moat + a reference logo.
3 · Two-stage optionality Near-term floor: a charging strategic / tier-1 / payments buyer at a Lynkwell/Flipturn tuck-in valuation. Scaled target: an EAM consolidator (IFS, Hexagon, IBM) buying the cross-vendor layer to complete "end-to-end asset lifecycle," at a Copperleaf-style valuation.
4 · Hold the position Vertical SaaS, not a horizontal platform, same product, better multiple, dodges the trenchcoat trap until you've earned the right to generalize.
